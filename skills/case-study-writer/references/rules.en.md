# Rules for honesty, citations and privacy

## Honesty
1. **Source or marker.** Every fact comes from a project document or stays as `[MISSING DATA: ...]`.
2. **Allowed calculations.** A percentage or difference derived from source figures is valid if it can be redone. State where it comes from (`3 of 5 people (60%)`).
3. **No superlatives without data.** "Much better", "remarkable" or "intuitive" do not count without a figure or a cited piece of evidence.
4. **Observation and interpretation kept apart.** "4 of 5 people abandoned" is an observation; "because they did not trust it" only if a source says so.
5. **Honest results.** If there is no later measurement, there is no "after". Say what was measured and what was not, and the limits (small sample, sample participants).
6. **The reflection belongs to whoever made the project.** Write it from what the sources and the person say went wrong or would change; if there is no material, ask for it or mark it missing.

## Citations
- Format: `[source: docs/testing/README.md]`. Several sources separated by commas: `[source: docs/brief.md, docs/prd.md]`.
- The path is relative to the project folder and must exist.
- In the full case, every sentence with a fact (figure, date, decision, finding) carries its citation. Bridging or transition sentences do not need one.
- An audit report is cited by its path and the figures come from its JSON block.

## Privacy and ethics
- Participants as P1, P2…; never names, nicknames, phone numbers or emails.
- No real employers, clients or third-party products, unless the source confirms permission to name them.
- Raw notes inform the context but are not quoted or repeated.
- If the data comes from a sample project, say so visibly (at the top of the case).

## Style
- Short, concrete sentences; one idea per sentence; action verbs.
- No filler: "in today's world", "an innovative solution", "it is worth noting".
- First person singular for the role and decisions ("I decided", "I tested"); plural only if there was a team.
